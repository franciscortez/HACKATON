# Ticket goal: Leveric engineering hackathon

Source: "Josh's private presentation" (20 slides, screen recording of 26 October 2026). This summary comes from the slide text. Slide numbers match the `slide_NN_*.png` files in this folder.

## Goal
Three engineers each build **one polished, working tool** that solves a recurring business problem with a complete, reusable workflow (slide 1). The prize is AUD250 cash (slide 1). The tool should be good enough to earn a place in Leveric's service offer (slide 3).

Quality beats quantity: finish one usable prototype before starting a second (slides 2, 17).

## What a winning entry needs (slide 2)
1. A real buyer: a recurring job, useful output and a reason to pay.
2. Two client contexts: reuse through configuration, not a bespoke rewrite.
3. Working evidence: fresh input, reliable output, clear costs and recovery.

## Candidate tools (slides 4-13, "ten chosen directions", slide 20)
Each slide gives input, output and a rule. Slides 7-13 are marked "Chosen direction"; slides 4-6 are marked "Website example".

| # | Tool | Input to output | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meeting to action | Transcript and names to action register and email draft | Leave unknown owners and dates unassigned |
| 2 | Sources to newsletter | Approved URLs, audience, dates to cited newsletter draft | Flag old or conflicting information; review before sending |
| 3 | Research to call brief | Company URL, contact, objective to one-page call brief | Separate facts from inferences; flag gaps |
| 4 | URL to social content | Public page, audience, brand to three posts and a caption | Stay grounded in the page; text outputs first |
| 5 | Brief to landing page | Offer brief, approved proof, brand to responsive page draft | Never invent testimonials or results; approve publishing |
| 6 | Source video to variations | Owned video, audience, hooks to three captioned variations | One reliable format first; disclose rendering cost |
| 7 | Enquiry to qualified handover | Enquiry and client fit rules to qualification and reply draft | Show reasons; no automatic rejection |
| 8 | Discovery to proposal | Discovery notes and approved catalogue to proposal draft | Approved prices only; flag missing details. Josh: this could be "next level for Leveric internally" and offered to other businesses |
| 9 | Client intake to onboarding | Template, intake, checklist to gaps, kickoff and welcome draft | Use supplied data; flag conflicts; isolate each client |
| 10 | Recording to SOP | Process recording or transcript to editable step-by-step SOP | Authorised recordings only; flag unclear steps |

Others: Brand and Brief Checker, Website Conversion Audit, Business Knowledge Assistant, Client Feedback Resolver (slide 20).

Slide 14 shows the pattern every tool follows (example: URL to content): add source and brief, extract and check, edit and approve, export approved content. A person approves; editing resets approval; no automatic publishing; a blocked source gets an explanation and a paste-text fallback.

## Commercial context, tentative (slide 3)
- Existing top tier: $10,000/month (six-month minimum, as stated by Josh).
- Proposed middle: about $7,000/month (exact offer undecided).
- Proposed entry: about $1,000/month (may be higher with operating costs).
- Prices exclude GST. Currency unconfirmed. Each entry contributes one candidate tool; the five-tool bundle is exploratory.

## Judging, proposed, Josh to approve (slide 15)
Score each category 0-5; points = rating / 5 x weight. Out of 100:
- Customer value and commercial usefulness: 30
- Functionality, reliability and demonstration: 25
- Reusability across different clients: 20
- User experience and output quality: 15
- Maintainability, cost and safeguards: 10

A second genuinely working tool can break a tie.

## Submission requirements, proposed (slide 16)
1. Working build: access instructions, source or export, reproducible setup.
2. Product note: buyer, recurring job, workaround, expected time saved.
3. Two client examples: both configurations and outputs, plus one failure case.
4. Cost note: cost per run, monthly use, assumptions, usage limits.
5. Short handover: architecture, checks, known limits, remaining work.

Use public or synthetic data unless client use is authorised. Disclose reused work. Keep secrets out of the handover.
